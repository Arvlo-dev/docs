---
title: Publishing Documentation
description: Learn how to publish, preview, update, and unpublish documentation in Arvlo.
---

Publishing makes your documentation available to your users. Before publishing, you can save drafts, preview your changes, and make updates without affecting your live documentation.

## Drafts
Every documentation page begins as a draft.

Draft pages are only visible to members of your project and can be edited freely until you're ready to publish them.

Drafts are useful for:

- Writing new documentation.
- Reviewing content before publishing.
- Collaborating with your team.
- Preparing documentation for an upcoming release.

Saving a draft does **not** make the page publicly available.

## Preview Mode
Before publishing, you can preview your documentation exactly as your users will see it.

Preview Mode allows you to:

- Verify formatting.
- Test links.
- Review images and media.
- Check custom components.
- Ensure your content is ready for publication.

Previewing your documentation before publishing helps catch formatting issues and broken links before they reach your users.

## Updating Existing Pages
Published pages can be updated at any time.

When editing an existing page, your changes are saved separately until you publish them. This allows you to continue improving your documentation without immediately affecting the live version.

Common updates include:

- Correcting mistakes.
- Adding new features.
- Updating screenshots.
- Improving explanations.
- Revising code examples.

After reviewing your changes in Preview Mode, publish the page again to make the latest version available.

## Unpublishing Pages
If a page is no longer relevant, it can be unpublished.

Unpublishing removes the page from your public documentation while preserving its content within your project.

This is useful when:

- A feature has been removed.
- Documentation is being rewritten.
- A page was published accidentally.
- Content is no longer intended for public access.

Once unpublished, the page is no longer accessible to visitors until it is published again.

## Best Practices
- Review every page in Preview Mode before publishing.
- Keep unfinished work as drafts until it's ready.
- Update documentation whenever your product changes.
- Remove or unpublish outdated documentation to keep your knowledge base accurate.
- Treat your documentation like your product—keep it current, polished, and easy to trust.
